PROJECT OVERVIEW

This project analyzes a full-funnel marketing dataset consisting of 1,000,000 records representing user behavior across the customer journey:

Impression → Click → Lead → Engagement → Purchase

The objective of this analysis was to evaluate funnel efficiency, diagnose performance, and determine whether the acquisition model is economically sustainable.

WHAT HAPPENED?

The funnel demonstrates strong performance across all major conversion stages.

Key Metrics

Click-Through Rate (CTR): 6%

Lead Rate: 35%+

Engagement Rate: 72%

Sales Rate (Lead to Purchase): 6%

Customer Acquisition Cost (CPA) is greater than Earnings Per Acquisition (EPA)

Top-of-funnel engagement is strong.
Mid-funnel conversion is healthy.
Bottom-of-funnel performance aligns with industry benchmarks.

However, CPA exceeding EPA indicates a profitability constraint.

PERFORMANCE EVALUATION: GOOD OR BAD?
Funnel Efficiency

A 6% CTR indicates effective audience targeting and compelling messaging.

A lead rate above 35% suggests minimal friction and strong value clarity.

A 72% engagement rate reflects aligned targeting and effective follow-up.

A 6% sales rate indicates the offer converts within strong benchmark ranges.

Operationally, the funnel is performing well.

Profitability Assessment

Customer Acquisition Cost exceeds Earnings Per Acquisition.

All campaigns are performing at similar efficiency levels.

There is no single traffic source underperforming.

This confirms the issue is structural unit economics rather than conversion breakdown.

STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATION

Since campaign-level optimization will not materially improve results, focus shifts to economic restructuring.

Increase Average Order Value (AOV)

Introduce product bundles

Add upsells or order bumps

Test pricing adjustments

Create premium tiers

Improve Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)

Implement structured post-purchase email flows

Encourage repeat purchases

Introduce subscription or continuity offers

Cross-sell complementary products

Improve Margin Structure

Evaluate cost of goods sold

Negotiate supplier costs

Reduce operational inefficiencies

If acquisition efficiency is optimized, margin becomes the primary profitability lever.

KEY INSIGHT

The funnel is operationally strong.

The constraint is economic, not behavioral.

Strong conversion metrics alone do not guarantee sustainable growth. Profitability depends on alignment between acquisition cost, revenue per customer, and margin structure.

CONCLUSION

The business is conversion-efficient but economically constrained.

Scaling under the current cost structure would amplify losses rather than increase profit.

Sustainable growth requires revenue expansion, retention optimization, or structural cost improvement.
